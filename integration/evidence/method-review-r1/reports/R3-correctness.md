## What is supported

* **Ownership-safe publication is a real mechanism with a real counterexample.** `Debugger/lib/Instrumentation/Passes.cpp:3020–3170` creates complete per-work-item vector slices or scalar stores under the sole publisher, and retains attempted/sample/capacity state. `Debugger/lib/Decode/Decoder.cpp:375–460` checks counts, sequence, tag and payload consistency. `artifacts/method-run/enflame/GCU-I3-evidence.md` distinguishes the original incomplete vector writer, the scalar repair, the later explicit-vector full matrix, and their very different costs. Numerical correctness alone did not reveal the trace defect. The completed-buffer tag is correctly not described as concurrent release/acquire publication.
* **Ascend hidden ABI and typed events are implemented.** `Profiler/llvm/AscendLateObserver.cpp:45–85,130–210` appends per-actor records and inserts issue/wait/checkpoint observations around existing primitives without adding a completion wait. Semantic filtering happens before timestamp insertion; program selection predicates publication only. `Profiler/python/flagtree_profiler/ascend_abi.py` derives formal roles from emitted compiler attributes rather than fixed offsets. The three-slot allocation is checked before allocation in `Debugger/python/Bindings.cpp:575–601`. The scope remains pinned A2 mixed actors, not arbitrary actor organizations.
* **Native identity crosses the actual worker boundary.** The versioned receipt header has explicit size assertions and ownership callbacks. `integration/patches/ascend/patch_launch_receipts.py:32–86` retains the sink across worker dispatch and publishes worker-local query statuses. `CannProfiler.cpp:2523–2603,2717–2779` protects lifetime, closure, duplicate publication, cancellation and failed queries. `cann_joint.py:_sealed_capture`, its context/compute joins, and `cann_native.py:_verify_source/_verify_dispatch` require capture lifetime, actual context membership and the compute-task foreign key. Task zero is correctly valid. This is considerably stronger than name/order/containment association.
* **Clock and replay boundaries are mostly faithful.** `cann_source_clock.py:36–143,147–267` checks exact compiled logger/SDK rate evidence and recomputed native proofs, adds local durations, and removes absolute placement. Neither task identity nor the 20 ns tick is used to fabricate a shared actor/native epoch. NVIDIA/GCU remain raw ticks. The old study plot is expressly conditional; it is not final-source clock certification.
* **Retention is implemented, not just promised.** `profile.py:957–1102,1153–1293` writes private recovery journals before sidecar completion/acknowledgement, uses atomic replacement and filesystem synchronization, and preserves error paths. `CannProfiler.cpp:2059–2104` rolls back SDK startup failure. The package honestly distinguishes recovery evidence from automatic cross-process resume.

I additionally performed a read-only check independent of the tool decoder on the actual retained `artifacts/method-run/ascend/longk-cost-r29-evidence-extracted/longk-cost-r29-1/capture/ai/observations.json`: all **33 buffers / 13,398 records** have matching raw-buffer SHA, actor headers, original record words and exported `20 × tick difference` duration. This confirms serialization/count/rate arithmetic for that bundle, not independent timing accuracy or a second numerical execution. The other full-archive audit results are retained evidence, not rerun by me.

## Findings and finite repairs

### R3-1 — Major; implementation gap: same-compilation readiness validation stops short of the claimed source binding

**Claim:** design mapping says the adapter validates the captured stream and source hashes before deriving typed readiness; `ascend-static-readiness-20261015/README.md` says it validates actual source SHA and compile identity.

**Source:** `ascend_hivm.py:29–62` verifies stream SHA and actor LLVM hashes, compares actor job compile IDs with the SDK receipt, but only copies `receipt['source_ir_sha256']` at line 42. Its signature receives no enclosing handoff identity or retained source bytes. `ascend_compiler.py:finish` passes the SDK capture and artifacts, without checking this source hash against the enclosing `source_snapshot.ir_sha256`; `execution_ascend.py:201–203` repeats this path. `test_ascend_hivm.py` even certifies its positive fixture without a source hash. The normal wrapper creates useful provenance, and the real retained capture may be correctly bound; that does not implement the stated rejection rule. A coherently stale capture/artifact set can remain internally consistent while belonging to a different enclosing source compilation.

**Repair:** pass the expected handoff compile ID and retained source snapshot into this validator; require successful enclosing handoff, matching receipt/job/enclosing IDs, and matching hashes computed from retained source bytes. Missing legacy evidence should produce partial/unavailable readiness. Retain exact captured input bytes or a bundle-relative immutable source artifact; avoid relying on expired `/tmp` paths. Add contracts for missing/mutated source, wrong enclosing compile ID, and a self-consistent but swapped capture/artifact set.

**Predicted validation:** the existing actual same-source fused fixture keeps its static channel; each swapped/missing/mutated case loses certified readiness while retaining raw evidence. This is finite provenance hardening, not a new research mechanism. I independently traced these checks after incidental peer-summary exposure disclosed above.

### R3-2 — Major; evidence gap: record fidelity and local rate conversion do not establish diagnostic fidelity under perturbation

**Claim:** the system serves operator optimization through semantic observations and reconstruction. The study's producer-before-consumer diagnosis motivates the implementation.

**Evidence:** late LLVM places two SYS reads around an existing primitive and logs immediately afterward. Raw audits verify bytes, counts, IDs and the duration formula, not whether the instrumented wait remains representative. `06-evaluation.tex:54–88` reports small RMSNorm device perturbation of 61–78%; reducing event count did not help. The long-K original configuration has low whole-task perturbation, which does not alone bound distortion of its short internal waits. In my inspected long-K bundle, **282 wait brackets** and **8,503 issue brackets** have zero exported duration; checkpoint zeros are expected. This is compatible with finite resolution and is not evidence of corruption, but it rules out treating those zero brackets as zero cost. Existing text correctly says so. The unresolved question is whether retained nonzero waits and their ranking support a reliable diagnosis, not whether every interval must be nonzero.

[KPerfIR §5.3 / Fig. 10](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi25-guan.pdf) explicitly studies distortion of asynchronous waits caused by profiling instructions. FlagPrism should address this measurement issue at its supported semantic scope, without copying that system's reconstruction algorithm or inventing cross-actor alignment.

**Repair:** first use already retained final-path captures to compare local per-actor wait summaries across the original and changed long-K configurations. Then perform only the missing controlled validation: a fixed-count existing-wait workload with independently varied producer work/readiness delay, an immediately-ready control, explicit empty-bracket/resolution observations, and math/count/reference checks. Compare the resulting local wait ordering with independent native/SDK observations without merging their clocks. Inspect final generated instructions around the measured primitive. Set an a priori resolution/error interpretation; report a failure or “diagnostically unresolved” result when logging changes the expected ordering. Do not manufacture a completion barrier or stretch intervals.

**Predicted validation:** increasing producer delay increases the intended consumer wait at fixed site/count, immediately-ready cases expose the measurement floor, and the configuration decision survives logging within the declared scope. If this fails, narrow the claim to structural/count/identity evidence or redesign the logger. Existing hardware is sufficient; new production users are not necessary for this experiment. No such experiment was run in this review.

### R3-3 — Major; evidence/expression gap: final service-to-optimization closure remains indirect

**Claim:** the introduction connects diagnosis to 1.62×/5.31× recovery and presents three mechanisms as a coherent service.

**Anchors:** the study expressly says its research observations and configuration changes do not themselves establish the general mechanism. Its source/actor plot uses a conditional shared frame. `06-evaluation.tex:90–108` leaves the formal optimization workflow as a TODO and otherwise establishes mechanism boundaries. Final functional manifests, held-out math, exact IDs and export costs are useful but cannot substitute for a final public-output-derived decision. This is a real missing evidential link even though the TODO itself is merely a drafting marker.

**Repair:** complete a compact reproducible evidence chain for the existing two cases: actual final public entry/request and binary → exported ownership or static readiness plus local observations → a stated decision → the already controlled profiler-disabled configuration result. Explicitly identify which study quantity the service cannot reproduce (dynamic iteration/buffer identity or shared source epoch), and show that the claimed decision does not require it. Reuse valid disabled measurements at their original versions rather than relabeling them final-source data. If existing captures lack an indispensable fact, collect that fact or reduce the service claim.

**Predicted validation:** an independent reader can infer the same worker/producer adjustment from the exported artifacts and trace each number to its measurement scope. This could be a short iteration if the retained captures suffice; it is not solved by adding more passing math tests or porting Proton/KPerfIR.

### R3-4 — Minor; implementation gap: Ascend typed-record validation does not cross-check descriptor semantics with a known site

`observations_ascend.py:65–75` accepts any supported semantic code, looks up `(actor, site)`, and attaches the manifest without verifying that the code agrees with its known primitive/semantics. For example a record bearing wait code 2 for a known `SET.FLAG.IMM` site is exported as a wait with `compiler_site_identified`. `Debugger/python/flagtree_debugger/api.py:1525–1541` then filters by that decoded semantic. This is a malformed/stale-record detection hole, not a demonstrated defect in actual device captures. Header consistency and enum validation do not detect it.

**Repair/validation:** when a unique authoritative manifest exists, require code/site agreement and the compiled selected program; reject inconsistent known-site records while retaining the failed raw capture. Keep absent/ambiguous legacy mappings explicitly partial. Add negative descriptor/program cases alongside current actor/header tests. No hardware run is needed to show rejection; a focused unchanged valid capture can check compatibility.

### R3-5 — Minor; expression gap: disclose default-mode compilation feasibility and reconcile version/status text

`06-evaluation.tex:14–21` correctly calls the full GCU matrix explicit-vector; it should also state that default scalar all-site validation reached six matrix cases before a **900 s compiler timeout**, whereas the uniform vector matrix took about **70.85 min**. The adverse compilation result is material to “practical” use and is preserved in `GCU-I3-evidence.md`, but largely invisible in the paper. Reporting runtime perturbation alone hides this operating constraint. A bounded one-store request is not a substitute for the default all-site feasibility result.

Public `semantic_profiling.md` also says Ascend's full matrix “remains pending” and static dependency extraction “is being completed separately,” then later records its completion. These are stale status statements, not proof the engineering was absent. The evaluation's final-regression TODO likewise survives the final common regression.

**Repair/validation:** add the scalar timeout and vector compile cost/boundary, and replace stale progress text with version-qualified support. A document-to-manifest audit suffices. Do not rerun whole matrices solely to erase honest earlier-source boundaries. Pending NVIDIA performance should remain pending under the explicit deferral.

## OSDI contribution assessment

The official [OSDI ’27 CFP, Submission Tracks](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi27/call-for-papers) permits Operational Systems papers without novel research ideas, while requiring substantive design, implementation, analysis/experience that expands public systems knowledge. It does not specify a production-user quota. The three research machines are the actual context; they must not become fictitious deployment history.

The generic ingredients are prior: [KPerfIR](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi25-guan.pdf) already supplies compiler profiling, bounded/circular recording, selectivity and asynchronous analysis across NVIDIA/AMD. [Proton's official CGO abstract](https://2026.cgo.org/details/cgo-2026-papers/23/Proton-Towards-Multi-level-Adaptive-Profiling-for-Triton) covers selective regions, custom metrics and vendor/instrumentation integration; I do not infer absent functionality from unavailable full text. [PASTA §III-G](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22103v1#S3.SS7) normalizes event semantics and timing metadata through backend handlers. The manuscript generally acknowledges these boundaries fairly.

The stronger contribution available here is tested systems knowledge: a logger's own lowered ownership can invalidate records despite correct math; caller/worker and dispatch/compute boundaries require distinct identity evidence; output bounds and low whole-task perturbation do not ensure practical collection or accurate local waiting. The ownership repair and retained-worker/context mechanism substantiate that knowledge. Typed readiness is useful but needs the provenance repair above. A schema, adapter count or engineering effort alone would not meet the contribution burden.

**Could the three mechanisms support this track? Yes, conditionally.** They address connected, concrete failure boundaries and have meaningful hardware evidence. **Could a short iteration fix the identified gap? Plausibly yes:** provenance/decoder checks and status text are bounded; the decisive work is final-service diagnostic closure plus a narrow independent wait-fidelity check. If those results do not support a useful diagnosis, more adapters or terminology cannot repair the argument: the minimum new condition becomes a logger/observation mode that preserves the needed decision, or a narrower paper centered on validated structural/identity failure lessons with convincing operational use. Current evidence does not justify declaring that structural failure in advance.
