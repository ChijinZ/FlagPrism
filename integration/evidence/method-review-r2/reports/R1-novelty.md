## Target and contribution standard

The official track asks for design, implementation, analysis and experience that expand public systems knowledge; new research ideas are not required. It still requires significance and clear advances over prior work, and the paper must stand alone without supplementary evidence. There is no stated production-user quota. Three research machines constrain external validity but do not by themselves exclude the work. [OSDI 2027 CFP, “Submission Tracks” and “Supplementary Material”](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi27/call-for-papers).

Under that standard, the strongest public knowledge is a set of experimentally grounded obligations:

1. A selected logical publisher can own only part of a vectorized record after lowering, even when the mathematical kernel remains correct.
2. An actual source invocation must cross the launch-worker boundary and be resolved to a unique compute member; matching stream/task numbers does not settle which SDK view represents computation.
3. Typed same-compilation dependency evidence and local observation counts can support a useful intervention even when source origins are partial and cross-actor clocks are unverified.
4. Trace completeness, whole-task perturbation, local-wait stability and end-to-end cost require separate checks. The retained failure of the local-wait test is substantive evidence about this implementation's operating boundary.

These obligations are connected by the actual service and case replays, rather than only enumerated as proposed principles. Their broader importance must nevertheless be argued more sharply in the paper.

## Closest primary prior and inherited mechanisms

**KPerfIR.** The primary paper already provides compiler-integrated semantic profiling across NVIDIA/AMD, region/loop information, selective tracing, bounded circular storage, hidden profile memory and producer/consumer analysis. Its §5.2 includes backend-specific collaborative publication on AMD; §5.3/Figure 10 explicitly discusses instrumentation-distorted asynchronous waits and conditional replay correction. Thus neither “logger mapping matters” nor “instrumentation can distort waits” is a first discovery here. The candidate contributes a different concrete ownership failure and an implementation-specific qualification result, not an automatic advance over KPerfIR's timing accuracy. [KPerfIR, §§4–5, especially pp. 213–214](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi25-guan.pdf); local primary `artifacts/method-run/prior-work/kperfir-osdi25.pdf`.

**Proton.** Its official abstract establishes selectable frontend regions, custom metrics, unified vendor/instrumentation backends and profiling across model/operator/compiler levels. The author LLVM slides add generic/target-specific lowering, collection modes, buffer/counter operations and compiler-linked producer/consumer traces (slides 5–17). These are foundations the candidate inherits or extends. I read the retained author slides directly; the final CGO full paper remains unavailable in the supplied finite search record, so I make no claim that it lacks the candidate's guarantees or mechanisms. [Official Proton abstract](https://2026.cgo.org/details/cgo-2026-papers/23/Proton-Towards-Multi-level-Adaptive-Profiling-for-Triton), [author LLVM slides](https://www.jokeren.tech/slides/LLVM25_slides.pdf); local `proton-cgo26-fulltext-search-20261015.json` records the access boundary.

**PASTA.** Its §III-G expressly normalizes equivalent event semantics, naming and timing metadata while exposing vendor-specific handlers. A common event schema, unavailable vendor-specific events and extensible adapters are therefore inherited concepts, not distinctions. The meaningful comparison is below that interface: the candidate's concrete compiled actors, publication and invocation evidence. Primary read: `artifacts/method-run/prior-work/pasta-cgo26-arxiv-v1.html`, §§III-B, III-D and III-G, especially HTML anchors `S3.SS7.p3`/`p4`; [primary version](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22103v1#S3.SS7).

The manuscript already credits much of this in `paper/sections/08-related-work.tex`. However, it does not yet make the two particularly close connections—collaborative publication and asynchronous wait distortion—explicit. Its NVIDIA-reference framing in the introduction is permissible as a study setup, but must not become a capability comparison that silently ignores other supported prior backends.

## Final assessment

**Functional usability: supported at the pinned, explicitly restricted interfaces. Diagnostic utility: supported for the two retrospective workflows, especially structural mapping and the first producer-granularity decision. OSDI acceptance: not established; significance and clear differentiation remain the central review risks.**

I find no unavoidable structural blocker for an Operational Systems paper centered on the existing implementation, its failed assumptions, and its qualified diagnostic utility. A finite iteration can address the main concerns: precise primary-prior comparison, three compact counterexamples, a usable-regime table, a clean version/evidence map and completion of the draft. This is more than stylistic cleanup, but does not require inventing a new profiler, deploying to production or porting the closest tools to new hardware.

If the intended contribution instead requires complete dynamic dependencies, shared-clock pipeline reconstruction, exact uninstrumented wait costs, or broad inexpensive fine-grained operation, the minimum missing conditions are real: compiler-carried dynamic identities, validated source/native/actor clock relation, an independently bounded local-perturbation method, and acceptable compile/device/host/export costs across the claimed regime. Those conditions are unavailable in the frozen candidate and cannot be supplied by finite text edits or green tests. They should remain outside this submission's claims.
