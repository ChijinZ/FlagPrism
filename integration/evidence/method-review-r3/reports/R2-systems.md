## Prior work and significance

The contribution cannot be compiler instrumentation, selectable regions, metadata association or multi-vendor normalization in the abstract. [KPerfIR §5.2–5.3](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi25-guan.pdf) already implements backend-specific collaborative AMD stores and analyzes asynchronous wait distortion with a conditional replay correction. The GCU publication bug and Ascend wait sensitivity are concrete new-environment qualification results, not first discoveries of those principles. [Proton's author/conference abstract](https://2026.cgo.org/details/cgo-2026-papers/23/Proton-Towards-Multi-level-Adaptive-Profiling-for-Triton) already covers selective APIs, vendor/instrumentation integration and multi-level context; unavailable full text cannot establish absent features. [PASTA §III-G](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22103v1#S3.SS7) already normalizes event semantics/timing and retains vendor handlers.

What remains is a coherent systems contribution: complete-record publication under the actual GCU value layout; same-compilation typed AIC/AIV readiness with conservative origin provenance; retained-worker/context/compute association across the evaluated CANN boundaries; and experimental evidence separating math, recording, identity, local fidelity and total collection cost. The manuscript's §7 explains reusable validation checks rather than merely listing adapter features. That combination plausibly deepens public knowledge of operational profiling in these environments. Its impact is narrower than a generally accurate cross-device timeline profiler, and OSDI reviewers may still judge its reach insufficient. That is a significance judgment, not a demonstrated missing implementation mechanism.
