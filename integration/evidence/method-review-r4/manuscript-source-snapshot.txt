\title{\Large\bf FlagPrism: Compiler-Assisted Fine-Grained Profiling\\
Across AI Accelerators (Operational Systems)}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Porting an optimized operator across AI accelerators preserves its mathematics
but can change its execution organization and the meaning of profiling data.
We present \sys{}, a compiler-assisted profiler for selective offline operator
diagnosis on NVIDIA, GCU, and Ascend. Its execution manifests, typed dynamic
observations, and invocation reconstruction preserve backend detail while
exposing incomplete attribution and unverified clocks. Two retrospective
workflows connect execution evidence to independently validated configuration
changes, with 1.62$\times$ and two-stage 5.31$\times$ recovery. Implementation
experience exposes incomplete logger publication under vector ownership,
ambiguous SDK dispatch/compute views, and local waits that change by
77--83\% between observation modes despite low whole-task perturbation.
These results support qualified structural and qualitative diagnosis, rather
than precise uninstrumented wait costs or a certified shared pipeline timeline.
\end{abstract}

===== paper/sections/01-introduction.tex =====
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Unified kernel frameworks let operator developers reuse program logic across
AI accelerators. For example, \compiler{} extends Triton to multiple
backends~\cite{flagtree}. We consider NVIDIA as the optimization starting point:
developers port implementations and configurations tuned there to other
accelerators. Performance still depends on how the target maps computation,
data movement and synchronization. Profiling must connect those execution
observations to the implementation being optimized.

Architecture diversity appears at several levels. GPUs with similar SIMT
models can differ in thread organization, local memory, resources and
asynchronous facilities~\cite{musaarchitecture,hopper}. Dedicated compute and
data-movement pipelines add different scheduling and synchronization
rules~\cite{ascendarchitecture}. Compilation can split, fuse, reorder or
pipeline a source region; observing an asynchronous issue need not measure
completion. A common interface alone therefore does not determine which
events to observe or how to relate them back to the program.

Existing work supplies important foundations. KPerfIR integrates fine-grained
profiling into compiler intermediate representation (IR) and analyzes intra-kernel execution and
overlap~\cite{kperfir2025}; Neutrino supports programmable instruction-level
probes~\cite{neutrino2025}. Proton combines vendor measurement interfaces and instrumentation
with multi-level program context~\cite{proton2026}, while PASTA provides a
modular analysis framework for accelerator workloads~\cite{pasta2026}.
These capabilities motivate a concrete question for a broader collection of
execution backends: which program mappings, instrumentation semantics, and
measurement assumptions can be reused, and which must change when the target
architecture changes? 
The study uses their NVIDIA information as its diagnostic reference, while
our closest-prior comparison includes KPerfIR's AMD publication and
asynchronous-wait replay mechanisms. The gap studied here concerns concrete
additional compiler/SDK boundaries, not an absence of fine-grained profiling
or cross-vendor support in prior work.

This paper investigates selective offline profiling for diagnosing and
recovering performance lost after porting. Structural mappings and qualified
instrumented observations support decisions; precise uninstrumented local
costs and a certified shared pipeline timeline remain outside our service. The resulting observations must explain
the logical operator region or execution behavior being optimized.
We examine three sources of difficulty.
First, compilation changes the relationship between logical regions and
execution instances. Second, instrumentation must respect the target's
execution semantics, including the distinction between submission, execution,
and waiting. Third, timestamps, recording mechanisms, and vendor observations
must be reconstructed into program-associated results without hiding
architecture-specific detail. The study examines targets with similar and different execution models.

We place an empirical study before the system design. Starting from
NVIDIA-tuned operators, it examines performance after porting and the portion
recoverable through target-specific changes. Two cases connect execution-level
causes to fine-grained diagnosis, implementation changes, and uninstrumented
performance recovery. They expose information that ordinary timing and
source-region observations leave unresolved.
The first case keeps a cooperative row-program organization while NVIDIA
warps and GCU vector work-items receive different mappings; the second unfolds
one fused tile into Ascend AIC/AIV producer--consumer actors. We do not classify
GCU as NVIDIA-like SIMT hardware. Target-local configuration changes recover
1.62$\times$ and two-stage 5.31$\times$ performance in the studied cases.
The final public service retrospectively reproduces the mapping clue and
first producer-granularity decision; the second fusion step uses instance
cardinality and independently measured task recovery, rather than lower waits.

We investigate \sys{}, a profiler whose system boundary includes the
profiling-related compilation mechanisms in \compiler{}, together with runtime
collection, event association, and analysis. Its service is to lower
common profiling requests into target-appropriate observations and reconnect
those observations to the operator program. It serves operator developers and
optimization tools through three mechanisms. A compiled execution manifest
retains specialization, actor, and surviving source relationships.
Execution-aware instrumentation produces typed dynamic observations with
explicit selection and recording limits. Evidence-based reconstruction binds
observations to actual invocations and preserves clock and attribution
boundaries. The implementation extends existing compiler passes, metadata,
and collectors; it does not require a new programming language. Missing source
origins and unverified cross-actor clock relations remain explicit.

The added public knowledge is the demonstrated trust failures, their concrete
implementation responses, and the diagnostic decisions that survive
qualification. The paper develops the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item An empirical study of performance loss after porting NVIDIA-tuned
operators, with execution-level diagnoses and targeted recovery under cooperative
program and producer--consumer organizations (Section~\ref{sec:study}).
\item A compiler--profiler implementation combining execution manifests,
typed dynamic instrumentation, and evidence-based invocation reconstruction
across NVIDIA, GCU, and Ascend backends (Section~\ref{sec:design}).
The implementation addresses concrete boundaries: ownership-safe record
publication, typed AIC/AIV readiness, and retained-worker association through
SDK dispatch and compute contexts.
\item Hardware-qualified validation of mathematical correctness, dynamic
recording, invocation association, and measurement costs on three backends
(Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}), including counterexamples that distinguish complete publication from correct
math, canonical compute tasks from SDK context views, and local wait stability
from whole-task perturbation and end-to-end cost.
\end{itemize}

The evaluation concerns three research environments and controlled operator
experiments; it does not establish production deployment or broad adoption.

===== paper/sections/02-background.tex =====
\section{Background and Profiling Scope}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Operators, Backends, and Execution Models}
We study Triton operator programs compiled through \compiler{}. Shapes, tiling,
worker requests and pipeline parameters specialize a program into a binary;
a portable parameter name need not select equivalent hardware organization.
The tested devices are an NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, an Enflame
ZIXIAOC200 GCU, and an Ascend 910B4-1. Their pinned compiler and SDK stacks
form part of the experimental target. We distinguish NVIDIA SIMT warps,
GCU vector work-items, and Ascend AIC/AIV producer--consumer actors; a vendor
label or the term GPU does not establish an identical execution model.

\subsection{From Program Regions to Device Execution}
A logical region is a developer-visible part of an operator. A compiled site
belongs to one binary, while a dynamic occurrence also belongs to one launch,
program and actor. Lowering can split, fuse or duplicate regions; a surviving
location may identify several candidate origins. Static ownership and
compiler dependencies describe organization, not measured utilization or cost.
We preserve these distinctions rather than requiring one source operation per
hardware event.

\centering

\caption{Conceptual profiling scope. Branches illustrate possible mappings,
not measured schedules. The study supplies concrete compiled examples.}
\label{fig:scope}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Measurement Semantics}
Host submission, native device-task execution, source-control brackets and
engine activity have different scopes. In particular, issuing an asynchronous
operation does not establish its completion; a wait bracket can change when
instrumentation redistributes work. Counters also require separate evidence
for their rate and epoch. Unknown-frequency GCU ticks remain ticks; supported
Ascend source counters provide actor-local durations without a certified
shared source/native origin. Independent SDK replays retain their own capture
identities and overlapping activities are not summed as exclusive wall time.

\section{Study: Diagnosing Performance Loss after Porting}
\label{sec:study}

\noindent\textbf{Story.} NVIDIA-tuned implementation $\rightarrow$
correct target port with different performance $\rightarrow$ execution-level
cause $\rightarrow$ backend-specific diagnosis $\rightarrow$ targeted change
and recovery.

\noindent\textbf{Setup.} Six operators; three shapes and two frozen NVIDIA
configurations per shape; RTX PRO 6000, ZIXIAOC200, and Ascend 910B4-1.
The source implements FP16-input/FP32-accumulation GEMM and fused GEMM+bias+GELU;
softmax, layer normalization, reduction, and transpose use FP32.
Identical input bytes $\rightarrow$ CPU-reference correctness checks
$\rightarrow$ saved compiled mappings $\rightarrow$ native kernel/task intervals.
All execution, tuning, and collection run in server Docker containers.
Backend compiler/SDK versions differ; clocks and exclusive device use are not
fixed. Results characterize these implementations and software stacks.
Uninstrumented and recovery timings below disable source-level observers;
the native activity collector remains enabled to identify kernel/task intervals.
They are not measurements with every profiling facility disabled.

\noindent\textbf{Optimization starting point.} A bounded NVIDIA search covers
1,152 configurations across 20 operator/shape combinations, including the two
long-$K$ matrix cases; 1,112 pass and 40 exceed shared-memory limits.
Resident CUDA-graph throughput $\rightarrow$ shortlist three distinct binaries
$\rightarrow$ three native batches of 30 calls per candidate $\rightarrow$
freeze the native-selected configuration. The scope includes GEMM warp
requests 4/8 and row/transpose requests 1/2/4/8; this is not exhaustive or
vendor-best tuning. Independent post-freeze captures supply the plotted
performance distributions and case comparisons.

\noindent\textbf{Coverage.} Compute, fusion, reductions, and data movement
$\rightarrow$ different shapes and source configurations $\rightarrow$

selection; configuration 1 is the next distinct native-shortlisted configuration.
Ports retain the requested configurations and mathematical operations.

\noindent\textbf{Interpreting large gaps.} Duration alone does not distinguish
hardware capability from an inefficient implementation path. For example,
GCU GEMM $512^3$ with the transferred $32^3$ tile and four work-items takes
approximately 94.74 ms; an independent device-stream measurement gives 94.76 ms.
The compiled per-item matrix is $16\times16$, with zero reported 2D instruction
share. At the same grid, one work-item produces a $32\times32$ local matrix,
positive 2D share, and 268.53$\mu$s. This sanity check identifies a software-path
contribution; it is not a chip-peak comparison or a universal tile threshold.
A $16\times32$ control also reports 2D activity, so local shape alone is not
a universal threshold. This check qualifies the overview; the two main cases
below provide more direct program-level diagnoses and controlled adjustments.

\subsection{Case A: Cooperative Reduction Mapping}
\label{sec:study-similar}
\noindent\textbf{Scope.} One row program and cooperating execution groups are
common to both ports; NVIDIA warps and GCU vector work-items have different
capacities and instruction organization. We compare this shared program
organization, without classifying ZIXIAOC200 as NVIDIA-like SIMT hardware.

\noindent\textbf{Observation.} Softmax $127\times769$, one row per program.
NVIDIA selects four warps: independent native medians are 1.152$\mu$s for four
and 1.248$\mu$s for one. The exact GCU port instead improves from approximately
115.06 to 71.12$\mu$s when the corresponding request changes four work-items to
one, with grid 127 and the input fixed: 1.62$\times$ target-local speedup.

\noindent\textbf{Diagnosis.} Timing ranks configurations but does not explain
how cooperation is organized on the target. Actual lowering exposes ownership
and partial exchange; this suggests testing fewer vector work-items, with the
prediction that a coarser per-item reduction can improve the port. Same request
$\rightarrow$ different ownership.
Actual NVIDIA indexing stripes columns across warps, assigning 193/192/192/192
useful elements; GCU assigns contiguous slices of 256/256/256/1.
GCU four-item lowering uses shared partial exchange and two barriers;
one-item lowering owns the full row and removes the exchange.
Original-binary replay counters show the SDK 1D instruction-cycle share rising from
0.841\% to 2.991\%. The share uses SDK total cycles as its denominator; it is a companion execution
characteristic, not GPU occupancy or an isolated explanation of the speedup.
Static element counts do not measure utilization or prove padding as the sole
cause.

\noindent\textbf{Controlled adjustment.} Hold source math, input, row grouping,
and grid fixed $\rightarrow$ change only the worker request $\rightarrow$
verify the changed mapping and three independent 30-call native batches.
A two-item control keeps both barriers but reaches approximately 73.65$\mu$s;
barrier presence alone therefore does not explain the four-item slowdown.
Ownership, local/shared resources, and worker-dependent synchronization costs
change together. The adjustment is validated; their individual cost
contributions remain unseparated.

\subsection{Case B: Producer--Consumer Granularity}
\label{sec:study-different}
\noindent\textbf{Observation.} Fused GEMM $512\times512\times4096$, a longer-$K$ shape
outside the overview, exposes producer--consumer behavior.
The frozen NVIDIA implementation uses $32^3$ tiles, four warps, and stage
request 4; independent native latency is 17.728$\mu$s. Its Ascend port takes
310.27$\mu$s. Each logical tile becomes one AIC producer and two AIV consumers,
connected by a global-memory workspace and readiness event 0.
The stage request enables the backend's multibuffering; it does not mean four
physical buffers.

\noindent\textbf{Diagnosis.} Long output-stage time $\rightarrow$ computation
or readiness? In SDK program 0 replays, AIV0 wait0 activity is 46.72$\mu$s
while vector activity is 0.293$\mu$s. Increasing only $B_K$ from 32 to 256 leaves
the saved AIV LLVM byte-identical, but reduces producer Cube commands 128$\rightarrow$16,
MTE2 activity 47.52$\rightarrow$23.81$\mu$s, and AIV0 wait0
46.72$\rightarrow$26.40$\mu$s; vector activity stays 0.293$\mu$s.
These activities may overlap and are not added to reconstruct wall time.
Sparse scalar-clock records expose iteration IDs, issue gates, and L1 buffers.
Within the retained study campaign, all three program 0 actor windows have
one compatible native-window match under a shared reported-counter assumption.
This is a campaign-level consistency check, not an independent runtime-identity
or counter-domain certificate. Figure~\ref{fig:study-actor}(b) conditionally
places the measured producer control and consumer wait brackets in that frame;
bars measure source control, not engine occupancy. Program 0 is one logical tile with three actors; these
local observations diagnose a dependency, while whole-launch time covers all
programs. They neither represent every instance nor reconstruct a launch
critical path.

\noindent\textbf{Reusable service boundary.} The study's sparse research probes
supply richer iteration/buffer details than the final public service. The latter
reproduces typed static readiness, local waits and producer counts, without the
conditional absolute placement. All-site versus wait-only collection changes
the original local waits by 77--83\%, while preserving the first intervention's
qualitative direction (Table~\ref{tab:wait-stability}). The second recovery is
supported by cardinality/loading evidence and independent native timing, not
by a stable further wait reduction (Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic-decisions}).

\noindent\textbf{Staged recovery.} Producer granularity first:
$32\times32\times32\rightarrow32\times32\times256$
$\rightarrow$ 310.27$\rightarrow$164.12$\mu$s, with identical output tiling and
logical input payload. The compiled instance and source-loading counts suggest output tiling next:
$64\times64\times256$ $\rightarrow$58.45$\mu$s.
Logical programs 256$\rightarrow$64 and source-requested A/B payload
128$\rightarrow$64 MiB; physical DRAM traffic is not inferred from this count.
The larger-tile case does not materially lower program 0 wait further: the second gain
comes with reduced instance cardinality and source-requested loading;
their separate causal contributions are not identified.
Held-out randomized native batches and CPU checks confirm the two-step
5.31$\times$ recovery. Additional $B_K$ and unroll explorations, including non-improvements,
remain in the artifact with their distinct configurations; they are not
all controls of the final frozen implementation.

\subsection{Information Needed to Diagnose the Loss}
\label{sec:study-information}
\noindent\textbf{Case A.} Total latency shows a target-side gap; source-level grouping
alone does not reveal striped versus contiguous ownership, shared exchange,
or vector resource activity. Compiled mappings $\rightarrow$ fewer-item hypothesis $\rightarrow$
controlled worker adjustment; per-SIP counters characterize the changed path.

\noindent\textbf{Case B.} A long output region does not identify the activity
responsible. Actor identity + readiness event + producer command granularity
$\rightarrow$ distinguish waiting from vector execution $\rightarrow$ improve
the producer before changing the consumer.

\noindent\textbf{Profiling gap.} Proton and KPerfIR supply NVIDIA profiling
information, including fine-grained regions~\cite{proton2026,kperfir2025}.
That information does not directly specify GCU vector ownership or Ascend
AIC/AIV readiness dependencies. Explaining these cases requires backend-specific execution information
associated with the source operator, beyond ordinary kernel or region durations.
Timing-only search can still find faster configurations; the demonstrated gap
is diagnostic explanation, not an impossibility of optimization without us. Our generic probes are not executions of those tools.

\noindent\textbf{Transition to the design.} Source operator/region
$\rightarrow$ actual execution instances and native activities
$\rightarrow$ optimization feedback. This motivates profiling services in
\sys{} with compilation support from \compiler{}: preserve backend detail and
its program association. The study validates research observations and
configuration changes; a general profiling IR or production mapping mechanism
must be substantiated separately in the design and evaluation.

===== paper/sections/04-design.tex =====
\section{Compiler-Assisted Profiling Design}
\label{sec:design}
\sys{} provides profiling feedback for operator optimization. A request specifies
observation semantics, programs, sites, capacity, and occurrence sampling.
Each backend produces the requested observations with explicit measurement
conditions or rejects unsupported requests. A common request does not require
different execution models to expose identical actors or clocks. The design
combines a compiled execution manifest, execution-aware instrumentation, and
evidence-based association and reconstruction.
The cooperative case requires ownership information both to explain the
operator mapping and to publish complete observation records. The fused case
requires a readiness channel, separate producer and consumer observations,
and a binding from those actors to the source invocation. These requirements
connect the three mechanisms; an interface that merely renames counters does
not supply the missing relationships.

\subsection{Mapping the Compiled Execution}
\label{sec:design-mapping}
Each actual launch links its arguments and configuration to a compiled binary.
Its execution manifest retains IR, backend organization, observable sites,
and surviving compiler-assigned operation or scope identifiers. Site IDs are
qualified by the binary; an ID in another specialization is not the same site.

Fusion and lowering can associate one operation with several sites, or retain
several source origins for one site. Adapters preserve available relationships
and distinguish unique, ambiguous, and missing origins. A compiler location
identifies an origin candidate, not exclusive cost ownership. An enclosing
region's duration cannot be independently assigned in full to each operation.
Backend actors and available dependency or buffer information remain explicit;
static relationships are not measurements of when an actor ran.
Runtime dispatches, device compute contexts, and compiler actors are distinct
levels. In the observed Ascend mixed execution, one dispatch has an SDK
dispatch-envelope view and a compute-context view; that compute context uses
both AIC and AIV actors. Treating both SDK views as independent kernels would
double count the execution.
The importer follows SDK context membership and the compute-task table's
explicit task reference. It associates a source invocation only when the
dispatch has one uniquely identified compute member, preserving its other
context views as dispatch evidence. Multiple compute members remain explicit
without assigning the source invocation to an arbitrary member. A context
number alone does not determine whether a task performs computation.
For Ascend, the adapter captures typed synchronization IR after the SDK's
graph synchronization solver, alongside the original and instrumented actor
LLVM from the same compilation. A synchronization site records its emitting
core kind, producer and consumer pipelines, and event flag. The adapter first
validates exact retained source and manifest bytes against their hashes,
the successful enclosing and SDK compilation identities, and the actor LLVM
artifact and completed driver job. Parsed manifest values must also agree,
including their types. Readiness and source-origin imports share this
validation; missing or stale evidence retains raw inventory without asserting
a verified association. The adapter then requires a unique corresponding actor in that compilation's LLVM inventory. It groups verified
sites by the typed event channel and creates an edge only for a unique
producer and consumer in different actor roles. Missing or multiple candidates
remain partial. Unresolved typed sites still count as competing channel
candidates; discarding their rejected or missing artifacts cannot certify
uniqueness. This recovers AIC-to-AIV readiness without guessing private
LLVM operand encodings. It is a compiler-static dependency, not a pairing of
dynamic flag generations.

With line information enabled, surviving LLVM locations are matched against
the retained source-operation snapshot. Exact locations can still identify
several origin candidates after lowering. The fused case validates the typed
producer--consumer edge and surviving site locations; its explicit source
operation IDs remain absent. The manifest consequently preserves both origin
candidates and unresolved associations instead of assigning exclusive costs.

\subsection{Observing Dynamic Execution}
\label{sec:design-observation}
Observations carry semantics, site, program, actor, occurrence, clock scope,
and collection provenance. A source-control interval measures execution
between instrumented control points, whose cost may differ from the
uninstrumented execution; an issue interval brackets submission; a wait bracket
includes control waiting; a checkpoint records a point. These are not
automatically completion times or engine occupancy. Instrumentation does not
add a completion barrier to manufacture a sequential pipeline.

Repeated visits append dynamic records rather than overwrite one static slot.
Backend-specific sequence and attempted-occurrence fields expose sampling
and capacity losses where supported. The decoder validates each publication
protocol; occurrence numbers are not assumed to be source-loop indices.
Snapshot and dynamic layouts are validated separately, retaining saturation
and incomplete-record reasons.

NVIDIA observes explicit profiling scopes with device program selection in the
supported single-CTA configuration; output site filtering does not reduce its
device instrumentation. GCU uses one selected program and a sole work-item
publisher. Its bounded-index implementation uses 32-bit trace arithmetic while
retaining the full SDK counter words, avoiding a global 64-bit arithmetic
change to the reference kernel. Publisher selection must also respect the
lowered value layout: selecting work-item zero does not guarantee that it
owns every word of a vector record. The default emits scalar words under the
selected publisher. The vector alternative repeats a complete record in each
work-item's ownership slice, then publishes from the selected work-item; it
does not partition a record across inactive publishers. This alternative
addresses SDK compilation feasibility, and its mode and actual configuration
enter the cache key. It does not imply lower perturbation. The final record
tag is validated after capture synchronization, not used as a concurrent
release/acquire protocol.

Ascend prepares a hidden observer argument before normal lowering and places
role-specific observations in late SDK LLVM. The runtime owns trace storage.
The allocation contract validates the final three-actor layout before any
device allocation. Formal hidden-only row and fused matrix--bias--GELU
captures preserve numerical results and produce dynamic records through this
path. The fused capture observes one AIC and two AIV actors for one selected
logical tile. Requested semantic classes are selected in the late pass before
insertion: unselected primitive brackets and loop checkpoints receive neither
counter reads nor records. Selected sites retain their ordinals from the
unfiltered enumeration of the same original actor IR. The effective selection
enters compilation identity and the manifest.
This is separate from the program predicate, which limits record publication;
counter reads at selected sites can still execute in other programs. Output
filtering retains the request boundary but does not itself establish lower
cost. Arbitrary site selection and occurrence sampling remain unsupported on
this path.
The six-family operator matrix additionally checks the complete path on
36 detailed configurations. Held-out RMSNorm checks varying shapes and
configurations; a long-$K$ fusion validates the same mechanism after tile
and reduction-step changes. These checks establish the tested recording
path, not universal event coverage.

\subsection{Associating and Reconstructing Feedback}
\label{sec:design-reconstruction}
Source mapping, invocation identity, and clock alignment are separate
obligations. Launch metadata identifies a source invocation and its actual
specialization. Native activity requires an independent invocation association;
grouping does not itself align timestamps.

On Ascend, the caller and SDK launch worker can differ. A retained per-launch
token therefore crosses a versioned capsule into a pure C++ worker callback.
Immediately after the original launch returns, the actual worker queries its
device, stream, and last task ID and publishes the original statuses with its
process and thread IDs. The callback needs neither Python's GIL nor an added
completion synchronization. Cancellation, duplicate publication, late
callbacks, and closed epochs remain explicit; zero task IDs are valid when
their queries succeed.

The collector seals capture lifetime through a unique newly created SDK
directory and matching process/device metadata. The importer requires a unique
native task and host-launch connection tuple within that capture. It does not
join by names, order, or timestamp containment. Multiple source receipts
naming one task remain ambiguous.
Four actual calls across two binaries and two streams validate the retained
worker and SDK connection-table path, including task zero.
The default row-kernel capture retains the complete SDK output and associates
four native tasks with their source invocations. Native copy, marker, and
synchronization records retain their own activity kinds rather than entering
the kernel hotspot table. A fresh fused capture additionally validates four
source-associated compute tasks while retaining their dispatch context views.

Clock descriptors retain scope, counter width, conversion evidence, and
alignment status. Instance-local ticks remain local without shared-domain
evidence; unknown frequency does not produce nanoseconds. Reconstruction
never stretches internal intervals to a native task envelope. That envelope
does not establish per-program occupancy or a critical path. Independent
counter and replay captures retain separate identities.

Rate conversion and epoch alignment are separate. For the supported Ascend A2
path, local conversion requires the exact source invocation and binary,
the retained device frequency, and matching compiled and SDK system-counter
instructions. A successful conversion supplies source-control durations while
preserving the actor-local clock. The raw SDK task-counter producer retains
packet bytes, offsets, and identities independently; matching a task does not
establish a common source-clock offset.
On this path one counter tick is 20\,ns; a zero-tick bracket does not establish
zero execution cost.
The present integration therefore exports these source observations without
a certified shared source/ACSQ origin. Native association and local duration
conversion remain useful, but do not authorize a cross-actor critical path.

Outputs preserve raw records, compiler artifacts, partial relationships,
and unavailable-quantity reasons alongside the reconstructed view. Developers
and tuning systems can inspect backend extensions without treating missing
information as a measured zero.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}
\sys{} extends \compiler{}'s compiler hooks and the FlagPrism native collectors.
The shared Python layer validates observation requests, records launch and
binary provenance, exports typed observations, and reconstructs associated
views. Compiler adapters extract execution manifests; backend passes and
launchers implement the supported observation semantics. Vendor collectors
retain native task and counter information as separate evidence.
The implementation inherits frontend launch metadata, explicit profiling
scopes, compiler pass infrastructure, and vendor collectors. It adds backend
execution adapters, the hidden-argument and late-LLVM observation path,
publication contracts, retained-worker identity and SDK-context validation,
and provenance-preserving reconstruction. The tested FlagTree baselines are
\texttt{57d348} (NVIDIA), \texttt{add842f2} (GCU), and
\texttt{0364ed4b} (Ascend), with backend integration patches retained separately.
The final shared runtime is \texttt{8fffdf6}; \texttt{1cb15d7} identifies
an earlier qualification snapshot. Backend manifests pin actual libraries,
integration patches and SDK dependencies. Full hardware matrices retain their
earlier campaign versions; the final provenance repair has a fresh four-launch
fusion check and exact-source Docker regression, not relabeled matrix timing.

\subsection{Backend Integration and Capability Status}
The NVIDIA implementation uses explicit Triton scopes and CUPTI native
activity. GCU combines compiler-assigned sites with the SDK global timer and
TOPSPTI tasks. Both have actual dynamic-record and operator-correctness tests.
The Ascend integration combines hidden observer storage, late LLVM role/site
instrumentation, native ACL profiling, and retained worker receipts. Its
generated worker and native task identity path pass four actual calls across
two binaries and two streams. A hidden-only row-kernel smoke preserves
numerical results and produces dynamic observations; local source durations
are separately validated against retained compiler and SDK evidence.
The default row-kernel export retains SDK files and 32 local-duration records
for four associated tasks. The fresh fused capture validates four tasks and
232 records across one AIC and two AIV actors. Both preserve actor-local
scope without a verified shared epoch. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} reports
the six-family, held-out and long-$K$ hardware campaigns with their own
versions; CPU contracts do not replace those device tests.
The final Ascend wheel preserves the validated compiler and two native
libraries, all 52 component Python modules, and the receipt ABI header;
isolated public imports and compiler/allocation guards pass. This is packaging
of accepted binaries, not a separate clean-source build.

\subsection{Limitations and Failure Handling}
Unsupported selection or semantic requests fail explicitly. Available source
origins can remain ambiguous, and unknown-frequency or local counters are
retained without fabricated global timestamps. The capture bundle includes
raw observations, compiler artifacts, native outputs, and file hashes.
Partial captures and original application errors survive cleanup failures.
Native observation export retains a recovery file before acknowledging queued
captures; atomic sidecar replacement avoids losing raw records on write errors.
SDK output cleanup follows successful copying and bundle export, preserving
the original session directory when export fails.

Native start failure rolls back session activation and collector ownership so
the caller can retry. Receipt contexts retain queued-worker lifetime and reject
late publication after capture closure. Independent replay captures are not
joined to an earlier invocation; graph, concurrency, and counter capabilities
remain backend-specific.
Detailed NVIDIA scopes require annotation and the supported single-CTA
configuration; device occurrence sampling is unavailable. GCU timestamps have
no certified frequency conversion. Ascend accepts one selected logical
program and compile-time semantic classes, but rejects arbitrary site
selection and occurrence sampling. Its source-to-ACSQ epoch relation remains
unverified. These restrictions are exposed rather than hidden by the common
request vocabulary.

\paragraph{Starting with a bounded request.}
On NVIDIA, begin with an annotated region, program 0, a bounded buffer and
the supported single-CTA configuration; inspect attempted/dropped records
before interpreting the scope. On GCU, inspect the binary-specific tracked
operation inventory, select one store and program 0, and use scalar publication
with capacity 64. The tested Softmax request completes; arbitrary all-site
compilation is not an established operating envelope. On Ascend, select one
logical program and existing wait semantics; reject unsupported arbitrary
sites or sampling. Check provenance, record loss and local clock status before
comparing an intervention. These are offline diagnostic requests, with the
separate compilation, device, host and export costs in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}.

\section{System Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
The study in Section~\ref{sec:study} characterizes the problem. We distinguish
functional regression on its operator families from held-out validation and
mechanism-specific tests. Reusing a study family for correctness does not
establish generalization to unseen operators. RMSNorm was selected for held-out
validation before the final experiments.
The final shared-source regression runs inside each server Docker; code and
native-library hashes qualify results from earlier hardware matrices.
Additional devices remain outside this evaluation.

\subsection{Measurement and Attribution Fidelity}
The GCU functional matrix covers six operator families, three shapes, and two
configurations, including tails. Its explicit vector-publication mode passes
all 36 cases with fixed inputs, original CPU-reference tolerances, input
immutability checks, and output canaries. Across 108 actual task captures,
the bounded all-site request retains 4,599 observations and declares 21,996
capacity-dropped records. These are bounded traces, not complete execution
histories. An independent artifact audit checks the 108 raw-buffer hashes and
the recorded case and request identities. GCU timestamps remain raw ticks;
this experiment does not certify frequency or cross-actor clock alignment.
The default scalar all-site path completed six matrix cases before a GELU
compilation reached the 900\,s limit. The explicit-vector matrix completed in
70.85 minutes. The latter establishes compilation feasibility for that mode,
not practical all-site collection cost or acceptance of the default path.

The Ascend matrix executes the same six families at three shapes and two
configurations in off, native, and detailed modes, for 108 requests. Numerical
results, input immutability, and output canaries pass in each mode. The 36
detailed requests produce 7,788 records from 216 per-invocation raw buffers,
with no declared capacity drops or counter saturation. All retained records
have local duration conversion and preserve instance-local clock scope.
Exact native task association is validated for the 72 native and detailed
requests; off-mode task identity is unavailable. These results establish
recording and association at the supported scope, not complete source-operation
attribution or a shared actor epoch.

Held-out RMSNorm adds three shapes and two configurations in all three modes;
all 18 requests pass the same numerical, input, and canary checks. The 12
native and detailed requests have exact task association, and each of the
six detailed requests produces observations. This is held-out coverage of
one family, not arbitrary-kernel support.
The frozen shared-source regression passes 491 tests on NVIDIA, 414 on GCU,
and 477 on Ascend; respectively 4, 5, and 20 tests are explicitly skipped for
opt-in browser or backend/fixture requirements. Focused hardware checks on
the preceding source include three NVIDIA selected-program requests and six
GCU calls across two streams and two specializations. The final provenance
repair adds a fresh four-launch Ascend fusion capture: numerical/input/canary
checks pass, and 34 unique source-location matches, 27 ambiguous and 13
unassociated origins remain explicit. Earlier operator matrices retain
their own version manifests. These checks establish functional regression,
not independent diagnostic timing accuracy.

\subsection{Overhead and Execution Perturbation}
In a controlled GCU Softmax experiment observing one selected store site,
three batches of 30 calls show 3.08--3.57\% median device-task perturbation
with scalar publication. The vector alternative incurs 26.47--26.95\% under
the same request. Scalar publication therefore remains the default; vector
publication provides an explicit alternative for SDK compilation feasibility.
Neither result establishes all-site cost. Detailed host invocation latency is about
5.2\,ms, substantially exceeding native submission, so the device-task result
does not imply low end-to-end overhead.

Ascend's all-site request is substantially more intrusive on the held-out
RMSNorm shape $127\times769$. Across three batches of 30 calls per mode,
native task medians are 6.33--6.60\,$\mu$s and detailed medians are
10.49--10.64\,$\mu$s, a paired increase of 61.2--67.6\%.
Detailed host invocation latency is 1.79--1.81\,ms, compared with
218--222\,$\mu$s for native collection and 79--85\,$\mu$s off.
The off phase has no isolated native task reference, so it cannot establish
native collection's device-task perturbation. The detailed result exceeds
our engineering target; bounded output and one selected program do not
establish low perturbation. Host invocation latency includes detailed
capture's synchronization and record handling; final bundle export is
reported separately.

Selecting only wait semantics in the compiler halves retained RMSNorm events
from twelve to six per call, with no counter reads at unselected sites.
However, three new 30-call batches still show 60.1--78.0\% device-task
perturbation and 1.71--1.76\,ms host invocation latency. Reducing event count therefore
does not establish a practical low-perturbation mode for this small kernel.
The profiler exposes this cost boundary rather than presenting the reduced
request as a performance improvement.

The preselected long-$K$ fusion has a different cost regime. For its original
$32^3$ output/reduction tile request at $512\times512\times4096$, three
batches of 30 calls give native task medians of 309.74--311.19\,$\mu$s and
all-site medians of 310.94--313.76\,$\mu$s: paired changes of
$-0.08$\% to $+1.30$\%. This meets the device-task target for that
configuration only. Detailed host invocation latency is 12.33--12.46\,ms versus
267--272\,$\mu$s natively; detailed export takes 15.62--17.21\,s and
produces 221--229\,MB per session of 33 calls including warmup. Consequently,
low device-task perturbation still does not imply low end-to-end collection
cost.
Registers, complete schedule perturbation and native-versus-off device cost
remain unmeasured here. We retain the known compile failures, high-cost
regimes and earlier contended NVIDIA results; clean-window NVIDIA performance
remains deferred. None is counted as a passed cost or accuracy gate.

\paragraph{Cooperative Softmax.}
Two bounded public captures use the canonical $127\times769$ inputs, one row

GCU ticks do not provide a microsecond breakdown of the recovery.

occurrence information; it does not reproduce the study's bespoke dynamic

The decision needs neither a cross-actor absolute timeline nor precise
uninstrumented wait costs. The service's contribution is the supported
execution explanation with explicit evidence and cost boundaries, rather
than automatic causal optimization.

\subsection{Mechanism Checks}
The publication repair provides a concrete ownership counterexample: the
original GCU vector record store preserves mathematical results yet loses
words when only one work-item publishes. Complete scalar publication and the
explicit complete-record-per-work-item alternative pass the byte and tag
oracles. Publisher selection alone is therefore insufficient.

For readiness, the original LLVM manifest retains encoded primitives without
inventing peers. The same-source typed SDK capture supplies the static
producer--consumer channel; print-only replay preserves actor LLVM bodies.
For reconstruction, repeated calls, different specializations and two actual
streams test invocation isolation. Ambiguous identities and unsupported clocks
remain unassociated in the negative contracts. These checks validate concrete
mechanism boundaries, not a comparative tool benchmark or measured backend
extension effort.

The evaluation covers the three available devices and their pinned software

===== paper/sections/07-experience.tex =====
\section{Engineering Experience and Scope}
\label{sec:experience}
The operational setting is development and controlled qualification on three
research machines, using the existing developer Docker containers and pinned
compiler/SDK stacks. We report integration failures and diagnostic workflows,
not production adoption, application speedups, or a user survey. The central
question is which apparently reasonable profiling assumptions failed and what
evidence a developer needs before using their output.

\subsection{Lessons from Backend Integration}
\paragraph{The logger has its own execution mapping.}
A mathematically correct kernel can publish an invalid trace. An early GCU
logger combined a single selected writer with a vector store whose lowering
distributed record words across work-items. Selecting one work-item left part
of the record unwritten. Inspecting the lowered stores exposed the mismatch.
In the retained failed four-item capture, 59 records have only their first
four 32-bit words written. The first record is
$[7,8,1,0,0,\ldots,0]$: its timestamp fields are unwritten, not evidence of
zero execution time. The nonzero-counter oracle fails despite correct math.
The repair changes the logger's ownership: scalar words are published by the
selected work-item, or the complete logical record is first made available in
each work-item's ownership slice. These alternatives have different costs. Trace validation must cover the actual writers and committed
record bytes as well as numerical outputs. A reusable check is to run the same
known-count kernel with one and several cooperating work-items, then verify
every committed record word and tag. Testing only the one-item case would
miss this failure. Passing the check establishes publication correctness;
it does not establish accurate interval costs.

\paragraph{Association must survive runtime boundaries.}
The Ascend launch caller and SDK worker can be different threads; caller-side
last-task queries therefore do not establish the launched task's identity.
The retained worker receipt crosses this boundary. A second boundary appears
inside the native output: a mixed dispatch has both dispatch and compute
context views. Their shared stream/task fields are insufficient to choose
one view. Context membership and the SDK compute-task reference distinguish
them while retaining both originals. Finally, temporary SDK output must
outlive native finalization so Python can construct and retain this evidence.
In a retained four-launch mixed capture, the verified import yields four
canonical compute tasks and retains four dispatch-context views separately;
the latter do not become additional source-kernel costs. Counting all eight
views as compute would report eight tasks for four launches. This is an
offline counterexample from retained views, not a measured legacy join or
another hardware experiment.
The resulting invariant is stronger than a matching task number: the launch
receipt, worker query, capture lifetime, and unique compute membership must
agree. A reusable test interleaves repeated and different specializations on
two streams, and separately removes or duplicates a receipt/context reference.
The first case must retain the correct source binding; the latter must remain
unassociated. Missing identity evidence therefore changes what the developer
can infer, rather than silently selecting the nearest native event.

\paragraph{Bounded recording is not a bound on perturbation.}
Small output and selected instances do not establish low cost. GCU all-site
Softmax instrumentation initially took about 4.65$\times$ the native device
task time. The later one-store scalar experiment has much lower device
perturbation, but detailed host submission remains about 5.2\,ms. NVIDIA site
filtering and Ascend program publication predicates also have distinct
measurement boundaries: discarded output does not imply that counter reads
or instrumentation were absent. On Ascend, compiler selection halves the
small RMSNorm record count, yet device perturbation remains 60--78\%.
The reusable validation is paired measurement of the unchanged workload and
each actual observation mode, keeping device, host, export, and storage costs
separate. When the cost gate fails, a smaller trace cannot certify a
representative local interval. The supported operating regime is selective
offline diagnosis, with the restrictions in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}.

\subsection{Scope and Threats to Validity}
Device comparisons include compiler/SDK differences, not microarchitecture
alone. Finite operator shapes and one held-out family do not establish
application speedups or arbitrary compiler-transform support. Partial origins,
actor-local clocks and independent replay scopes retain their limits.
NVIDIA functional tests do not resolve its deferred performance gate.
Association and complete records are necessary but insufficient for faithful
local timing: original fusion waits change by 77--83\% between modes despite
low whole-task perturbation. Only the first intervention's qualitative
direction survives; the second relies on cardinality and independent timing.

===== paper/sections/08-related-work.tex =====
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Compiler and instruction-level profiling.}
KPerfIR~\cite{kperfir2025} embeds programmable profiling in the compiler
pipeline and supports fine-grained intra-kernel analysis.
Neutrino~\cite{neutrino2025} provides programmable probing.
Proton~\cite{proton2026} combines vendor and instrumentation backends with
selective regions and custom metrics. These systems provide foundations for
this work; compiler insertion, circular recording, selectable metrics, and
multiple vendor backends are not by themselves our contributions.

KPerfIR already relates compiler regions, iterations, and asynchronous behavior;
these are not distinguishing capabilities in the abstract. Its Section~5.2
uses backend-specific collaborative publication on AMD, and Section~5.3
analyzes instrumentation-distorted asynchronous waits with conditional replay
correction. We reuse the premise that logging and timing require execution
semantics. Our GCU counterexample concerns a sole selected writer that owns
only four of sixteen record words after vector lowering. Our Ascend controls
expose mode-dependent wait redistribution but provide no improved general
replay correction: they preserve only a qualitative decision. Retained worker
receipts and compute-context membership address a separate invocation boundary
in the evaluated SDK. These are concrete implementation and qualification
results, rather than first discoveries of collaborative logging or wait distortion. Our implementation
examines concrete boundaries encountered in the evaluated compiler and SDK
stacks. It validates full-record publication under GCU work-item ownership, extracts
typed AIC/AIV readiness from the same SDK compilation, and associates CANN
source invocations through actual worker receipts and SDK context membership.
These are the concrete integration mechanisms evaluated here; we do not claim
the first compiler-assisted profiler, semantic normalization, or multi-vendor
interface. Nor do we infer that earlier tools provide only coarse counters.

\paragraph{Context and accelerator analysis.}
DeepContext~\cite{deepcontext2025} connects program contexts across the deep
learning stack, and PASTA~\cite{pasta2026} supplies modular accelerator analysis.
PASTA normalizes event semantics and timing metadata while retaining
vendor-specific handlers. Event normalization and backend extensions therefore
also form part of the prior infrastructure; our comparison must identify the
additional supported compiler mappings and observation-association mechanisms.
Broader workload-context analysis complements this scope: our validation
addresses compiled operator observations and their attribution boundaries,
not complete application or distributed-training diagnosis.

===== paper/sections/09-conclusion.tex =====
\section{Conclusion}
\sys{} connects compiled execution organization, typed dynamic observations and
actual invocation evidence for selective operator diagnosis on three backend
stacks. Two retrospective workflows reproduce useful structural and qualitative
optimization decisions with independent recovery measurements. The most useful
systems lessons concern the logger's own ownership, worker/context identity,
and the separation of record correctness, local fidelity and collection cost.
Partial origins, mode-sensitive waits, expensive collection and unverified
shared clocks remain visible limits of the implemented service.
